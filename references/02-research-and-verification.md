# Research and verification

## The category test

**The most common failure in this genre is mixing categories.** A published
"top 10 companies" list will happily rank an audit firm, an infrastructure
platform and a development agency against each other by hourly rate, as though
a buyer could choose between them.

Before comparing anything else, sort every vendor into what it actually sells:

- **Build partners** — you hire them to write the thing. They bill hourly or by
  project.
- **Auditors** — they review what someone else built. Scoped per engagement, no
  hourly rate.
- **Protocol / research firms** — they build chains, clients, bridges. Priced
  per engagement.
- **Infrastructure platforms** — you subscribe. Usage-based, not hourly.

A firm that does not sell hourly development gets **no hourly figure**. Write
"no public hourly rate (audit engagement)" and say why. Any table that puts one
hourly number in all ten rows invented several of them, and that is a line worth
putting in the article.

## Source tiers

| Tier | What | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The directory of record (Clutch or equivalent) | Founding year, headcount band, rate band, minimum project size, headquarters |
| 1 | A company's own official statement or post | What that company's position is |
| 2 | Named research firm with report year | Market size and growth figures |
| 3 | Established trade press | Events, closures, acquisitions |
| — | The vendor's own marketing page | **Not a source for its own figures** |

## Verify one profile at a time

Not a bulk scrape. Open each profile, read the five fields, record them with the
check date. On the source batch, verifying 50 firms individually found that
roughly **80% of the figures published about them elsewhere were wrong** — and
those corrections became the article's main value.

Findings that only individual checking produces:

- A platform ranked #1 in most 2026 lists had reached **end of support in April
  2023**, with its flagship project wound down. It cannot be bought as described.
- A firm routinely published at 1,000–5,000 employees verified at 250–999.
- Two founding years were wrong by four years.
- Five firms were published above their verified rate band — one at $70–$150
  against a verified $25–$49.
- An audit contest platform recommended in two drafts had **closed**, directing
  its work elsewhere. It was still being recommended by the guides being
  rewritten, and by the first draft of the rewrite.
- Five companies in one conclusion did not exist.

That last one is the reason for the count gate in Phase 3.

## When sources disagree

State the disagreement in the entry; do not resolve it silently. Show the
directory figure as the number, and say what the firm publishes about itself.
"Its verified band is 250 to 999, against the 1,000 to 5,000 routinely
published" is more useful to a buyer than either figure alone.

## Statistics

Every market figure carries the publishing firm and the report year. Where two
credible firms disagree wildly, **say so and say why** — one article's opening
notes an eighty-fold gap between two 2026 forecasts for the same market, because
one counts blockchain-game revenue and the other counts addressable gaming
spend. That paragraph is better content than either number quoted alone.

Check the age of anything you half-remember. A headcount figure written from
memory was two years stale in one draft; the correct current figure was on the
company's own filings.

## Community signal

Reddit and X are useful for buyer experience and for a company's own public
statements, and not much else. Two rules:

- A platform's own account stating its position is a **tier 1** source for what
  that position is.
- Anonymous complaint threads are not a source for a factual claim about a
  vendor. Use them to find questions worth answering, then verify the answer.

Do not paste API keys into a prompt. Keys belong in a config file the script
reads.
